## Before — invented rough draft

When we decided to run a community workshop, the first thing we done was order some supplies. This seemed sensible at the time, but it caused a few problems later on. The workshop was about making notebooks from paper that would otherwise have been thrown away, and everybody had a slightly different idea of what that meant. Mara imagined a quiet afternoon with twelve people, while Leon had already told twenty neighbours that there would be a place for them. We hadn't actually checked how many chairs were in the room. There were six volunteers involved, and they each assumed somebody else was keeping a list of who was coming. At our next meeting, we wrote the purpose of the workshop on a large sheet of paper: make one simple notebook and learn a method you can use again at home. We then made three lists, one for materials, one for jobs, and one for questions that still needed answering. The room was free, except for the cleaning charge, which wasn't in our budget. Mara agreed to check this. Having moved the supplies into the cupboard, the table was finally clear enough for us to lay out a trial set of materials.

## After — edited text

When we decided to run a community workshop, the first thing we did was order supplies. This seemed sensible at the time, but it caused problems later. The workshop was about making notebooks from paper that would otherwise have been thrown away, and everyone had a slightly different idea of what that meant. Mara imagined a quiet afternoon with twelve people, while Leon had already told twenty neighbours there would be a place for them. We hadn't checked how many chairs were in the room.[AQ1] Each of the six volunteers assumed someone else was keeping a list of attendees.

At our next meeting, we wrote the workshop's purpose on a large sheet of paper: make one simple notebook and learn a method you can use again at home. We then made three lists: materials, jobs, and unanswered questions. There was no room hire fee, but the cleaning charge wasn't in our budget. Mara agreed to check the charge.[AQ2]

Once the supplies had been moved into the cupboard, the table was clear enough for us to lay out a trial set of materials.

## Editorial notes

- **Grammar:** corrected “we done” to “we did” and repaired the dangling modifier in the final sentence. The edit leaves the person who moved the supplies unspecified.
- **Clarity and concision:** shortened repetitive phrasing, clarified the three lists, and replaced the vague “this” with “the charge,” subject to author confirmation.
- **Meaning and voice:** retained the first-person account, contractions, sequence of events, and differing attendance expectations. The room charge remains unresolved.
- **Paragraphs:** separated the initial planning problem, the group's response, and the practical setup step to make the progression easier to follow.

## Author queries

1. **AQ1 — Is a step missing?** The draft mentions twelve expected participants, twenty promised places, and an unchecked chair count. Does the next passage explain how capacity and attendance were reconciled? If not, consider adding that outcome here; no resolution has been invented.
2. **AQ2 — What did Mara agree to check?** The edit assumes she would confirm the cleaning charge. Did you instead mean she would check the overall budget or whether the charge could be waived? Please confirm before accepting this change.
