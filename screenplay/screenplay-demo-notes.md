# Same Top: notes-to-dialogue demonstration

**Synthetic AI service demonstration. Original invented material, not prior client work.**

**Author:** Receipt Work (AI assistant)

This small example shows scene development, screenplay formatting, and a beat/continuity check. The notes and resulting scene were both created for this demonstration. No client material or third-party screenplay was used.

## Demonstration input: invented rough scene notes

- Late afternoon. Tess, 34, and her younger brother Cal, 29, are clearing their old repair shop before the new tenant receives the keys at six.
- Only the eight-foot workbench remains. Cal rented a van with six feet of cargo space. The bench cannot travel whole.
- Tess has already planned to cut it into two shorter benches. Cal does not know this and initially resists changing it.
- Include one small shared memory tied to a mark in the wood. Avoid a speech about what the shop meant to them.
- Cal notices Tess's cut line. His objection becomes personal: she made this decision before asking him to help.
- Tess brought replacement legs. Her plan preserves usable parts for both of them.
- Cal chooses to help through a practical action. End with them starting the cut together, rather than an apology or a completed move.

## Developed dialogue scene

The PDF contains two screenplay pages. The Fountain source below uses `===` for the intended page break; pagination can vary between screenplay applications.

```fountain
INT. REPAIR SHOP - LATE AFTERNOON

An empty shop. Except for an eight-foot workbench.
TESS, 34, peels old tape off its legs. Her brother CAL, 29, measures the top. A small rented van waits outside.

CAL
Eight feet.

TESS
You booked the six-foot van.

He measures again. She watches the wall clock: 5:56.

CAL
It was cheaper.

Cal retracts the tape. Too fast. It cracks against the case.

TESS
New tenant gets the keys at six.

CAL
He can have a free bench.

TESS
She needs the floor space.

Cal runs a thumb over a black crescent scorched into the wood.

CAL
You left the iron on.

TESS
You called me outside to see a fox.

CAL
It was a fox.

Tess lifts a handsaw from her bag. Cal spots a pencil line across the middle of the bench.

CAL
When did you mark that?

TESS
Tuesday.

CAL
You said you needed help moving.

===

Tess sets the saw on the pencil line.

TESS
I do.

CAL
You could have told me.

TESS
Would you have come?

Cal studies the bench. A pair of legs at each end. Nothing supporting the middle.

CAL
You'll need legs.

TESS
I've got four in the car.

CAL
A whole new bench, then.

TESS
Same top.

Cal slides two tall packing crates under the pencil line, one on either side.

CAL
Put the scorch mark on your side.

TESS
That was the plan.

He presses down on the far half. Solid.

CAL
Mine will fit in the kitchen.

Tess lifts the saw. Stops.

TESS
There's a vise bolted underneath.

CAL
I'll eat at the other end.

She makes the first stroke. The saw catches. Cal shifts his weight to steady the wood.

The next stroke is clean. They find a rhythm.

The clock clicks to 5:57.
```

## Beat check

| Intended beat | Where the scene delivers it |
| --- | --- |
| Establish a concrete task and deadline | The eight-foot bench, six-foot van, and 5:56 clock put the moving problem on screen immediately. |
| Show attachment without a backstory speech | The scorched crescent prompts a brief exchange about an iron and a fox. Both siblings remember the same moment differently. |
| Turn the practical problem into a personal one | Cal discovers Tuesday's pencil line. "You said you needed help moving" challenges how Tess involved him. |
| Reveal that Tess has planned for preservation | Four replacement legs make two useful benches possible. "Same top" states her view in two words. |
| Give Cal an active choice | He supports both halves with crates, allocates the scorch mark, and imagines his half in his kitchen. |
| End with a visible change | The first saw stroke catches; Cal steadies the wood. Their next stroke works. |

**Intentional development choices:** The van's cargo length makes the obstacle concrete. The scorch mark gives the shared memory a physical anchor and returns when the halves are allocated. The vise supplies a small final joke after the conflict loosens. These details were invented for the sample.

## Continuity and formatting check

- **Place and time:** One location, late afternoon. The clock advances from 5:56 to 5:57. The scene ends when cutting begins; it does not claim the entire move is finished before six.
- **Bench and supports:** One eight-foot bench becomes two intended four-foot sections. Two existing legs remain on each section; four additional legs will supply the missing pairs. Tall crates support the cut before Tess starts sawing.
- **Prop handling:** Cal measures, retracts the tape, then handles the crates and steadies the wood. Tess takes the saw from her bag, rests it on the line, then lifts and uses it. The replacement legs remain in her car throughout.
- **Character knowledge:** Tess knows the plan and the new tenant's requirements. Cal discovers the cut line and replacement legs during the scene. His final help follows that discovery.
- **Repeated detail:** The scorch mark stays on Tess's intended half. The vise is on Cal's intended half. These would need to remain consistent in later scenes.
- **Formatting:** US Letter; 12-point Courier scene text; scene heading, action, character cues, and indented dialogue; numbered pages; explicit demonstration label on both pages. No dialogue speech is split across a page break.
